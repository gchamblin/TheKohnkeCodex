Break-In Checklist (First 5 Hours)

The first 5 hours set the tone for the engine's entire life. Follow this protocol exactly.

Done Phase Required Action
Before Start Check oil (SAE 10W-30), verify fresh fuel, set rear tires (10-12 PSI), tighten all loose bolts.
First 15 Mins Run at half throttle for initial 15 minutes. Avoid full-load heavy mowing.
Hours 1–5 Mow lightly. Inspect for oil or fuel leaks after every pass.
At 5 Hours (MANDATORY) Change engine oil immediately to flush metal wear particles. Re-torque deck bolts, inspect belt tension, grease zerks.
⚠ CAUTION: Do NOT skip the 5-hour break-in oil change. Leaving initial wear particles in the crankcase accelerates engine wear.

Cleaning, Care & Pro Operating Tips

Cleaning & Care Rules

  • • Blow Off Post-Mow: Use leaf blower or compressed air. Never pressure wash spindles (destroys grease seals).
  • • Hydro Fan Maintenance: Keep clear in FL heat. Scrape deck underside while clippings are fresh.
  • • Fuel Care: Use fresh unleaded every 30 days. Add Sta-Bil fuel stabilizer if sitting >30 days.
  • • Storage & UV Protection: Store covered in shed/tarp. Wipe vinyl seat with UV protectant against FL sun.

Hard-Won Operating Rules

  • • Sharp Blades & Dry Grass: Sharpen every 50 hrs. Never mow wet grass (causes clumping and deep ruts).
  • • The 1/3 Rule: Never cut off more than 1/3 of grass blade height in one pass to avoid stressing turf.
  • • Alternate Patterns: Change mowing directions weekly to prevent severe soil compaction and ruts.
  • • Vibration Warning: Excessive shaking = bent blade or loose spindle. Shut off immediately!
Did You Know? Clippings are 80-85% water. Dried clippings trap moisture against metal like concrete, accelerating deck rust. Scrape weekly!

St. Augustine Grass Management
  • • Cutting Rules: Maintain height at 3.5–4 inches (never below 3"). Never cut off >1/3 height. Raise deck to 4" in July/Aug heat to shade root system.
  • • Watering & Feeding: 1 inch/week early morning (before 10 AM). Fertilize April, June, and Sept only. Never fertilize Oct–March.
  • • Pests & Fungus: Watch for Chinch bugs in dry brown patches. Brown patch fungus comes from evening watering — keep watering to mornings!

Cub Cadet & International Harvester — The Full Story

Every Cub Cadet, including John's, traces back to a Louisville, Kentucky factory floor in 1960. Here's the timeline from "why does IH make a tiny tractor" to the zero-turn sitting in the garage today.

1960
A Premium Idea at International Harvester
International Harvester (IH), already famous for Farmall farm tractors, greenlights a new line of small, heavy-duty lawn & garden tractors out of its Louisville, KY plant — aimed at suburban homeowners who wanted "real tractor" durability, not a toy mower.
1961
The Original Cub Cadet
The first Cub Cadet (Model 100 / "Original") ships with a 7-hp Kohler engine and a cast-iron rear end and drive shaft borrowed straight from IH's full-size Farmall Cub tractor. Early units with round fenders and cast-iron reduction housings are now the most prized collector pieces of the whole line.
1963
Shaft-Drive Arrives
IH introduces its first shaft-drive Cub Cadet model, improving reliability over the early belt-driven setups — another step toward "built like a farm tractor."
Why Yellow & White? IH deliberately chose yellow-and-white for Cub Cadet instead of "Farmall Red," so the little homeowner tractors would never be confused with its commercial farm equipment lineup.
1966
Hydrostatic Drive Pioneer
The Model 123 becomes the first garden tractor on the market with a hydrostatic transmission — no clutch, no gears, just push a pedal. This is the direct ancestor of the clutchless hydro drive still used in John's zero-turn today.
1971–1980
The Wide-Frame & Quietline Era
IH rolls out its best-loved workhorse models: the 149 (wide-frame, hydraulic deck lift), the 1650 Quietline (rubber-isolated engine mounts to cut vibration/noise), and in 1974 the ultra-rare Model 169 — only ~4,000 built in a 5-month run, packing the highest horsepower single-cylinder engine IH ever put in a garden tractor.
1976
The "Spirit of '76" Bicentennial Special
A limited commemorative model built for the U.S. Bicentennial — white paint, red/blue decals, and star graphics. Unrestored originals are among the most valuable Cub Cadets a collector can find today.
1979–1981
The Red-and-Black Era & the "Super Garden Tractor"
Model 782 introduces the iconic red-and-black IH color scheme with twin-cylinder power. The Model 982 follows as the original "Super Garden Tractor" — a stretched frame with power steering and a true 3-point hitch, aimed at users who wanted a mini farm tractor, not just a mower.
1981
Sold to MTD
Financial trouble forces International Harvester to sell the Cub Cadet division to MTD (Modern Tool & Die), which forms the Cub Cadet Corporation and shifts manufacturing to Brownsville, Tennessee. This ends the 20-year "IH era" that vintage collectors chase today.
1990s–2000s
Zero-Turn Innovation
Cub Cadet pushes hard into residential and commercial zero-turn mowers, eventually launching its Z-Series (1997) and pioneering steering-wheel-controlled, 4-wheel-steer zero-turns (Synchro-Steer) so operators could handle steep hills without dual lap bars — the same basic zero-turn concept John drives today, minus the "aero-mow" excuses.
2021
Stanley Black & Decker Takes Full Ownership
Stanley Black & Decker acquires full ownership of MTD, bringing Cub Cadet under the same corporate roof as DeWalt and Craftsman.
2026
John Kohnke Joins the Story
John buys his own Cub Cadet zero-turn (model 17BIEAEE010, 21.5 HP) and — after a few enthusiastic Synchro-Steer moments in front of Cathy — earns his place in Cub Cadet lore.
